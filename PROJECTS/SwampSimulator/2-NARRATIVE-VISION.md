# Narrative / Thematic Vision

## OVERVIEW

SwampSimulator is a calm, observational sandbox where a wetland comes alive on screen and runs by itself. The player is a quiet witness to a self-regulating world — a frog snaps at a mosquito, a heron stalks the shallows, an alligator slides through the duckweed. Beneath the surface, every species depends on every other species in a chain that the player can see, trace, and disturb.

The product sits at the intersection of three traditions: the agent-based ecology classics (SimEarth, the early Maxis "soup" experiments), the meditative idle-toys of the modern indie web (Cell to Singularity, Universe Sandbox), and the curriculum-aligned interactive science demos (PhET, Concord Consortium). It rejects the gamified-progression treadmill of most simulators in favor of a slow, narratively-rich loop: notice something, ask why, intervene, watch the consequences ripple.

The core narrative arc is **interdependence**. The swamp is a story told in food chains. Remove a single thread and the whole tapestry shifts in visible, measurable ways. Restore it and watch balance return — sometimes. Sometimes the damage is permanent. The simulation refuses to be a power fantasy; it is, instead, a lesson in humility delivered through animated cattails and absent-minded turtles.

## COMPETITIVE & CREATIVE RESEARCH

**Direct comparators:**
- *Equilinox* (Glitchbyte, 2018) — relaxing nature simulator with species unlocks and biome stewardship. Closest tonal precedent. SwampSimulator differs by focusing on a single biome with deep ecological accuracy rather than a broad biosphere with light mechanics.
- *Eco* (Strange Loop Games, 2018) — multiplayer survival sim with real ecological collapse mechanics. Inspirational for cascade modelling. SwampSimulator borrows the idea that *every action propagates*, but strips out crafting, multiplayer, and player avatars.
- *SimEarth* (Maxis, 1990) — pioneer of planetary agent ecology. Inspirational for letting the world run unattended. SwampSimulator inherits the "set it up and watch" loop while modernising the visual language.

**Adjacent inspirations:**
- *Mountain* (David O'Reilly, 2014) — proves that a non-interactive, observational digital toy can hold attention for hours.
- *Everything* (David O'Reilly, 2017) — establishes that scale-shifting (zoom from herons to mosquito larvae) creates emotional engagement with non-human entities.
- *Wattam* (Funomena, 2019) — joyful interconnection as core mechanic; everything is a friend, everything matters.
- David Attenborough's *The Green Planet* and *Our Planet* — visual reference for the "every organism has a job" framing that underpins the entire educational thesis.
- E.O. Wilson, *Half-Earth* — philosophical underpinning: biodiversity is not aesthetic, it is structural.

**What the market lacks (the gap SwampSimulator fills):**
1. A focused single-biome simulator that models *a real working food web* rather than abstract resource icons.
2. A simulation where the *food web is the UI*, not buried in a stats screen.
3. A teaching tool that demonstrates trophic cascades through emergent agent behaviour, not scripted cutscenes.
4. A peaceful, single-player, no-progression observational experience appropriate for both classroom and casual personal use.

## THEMES AND TONE

**Primary themes:**
1. **Interdependence** — no organism is ornamental; every species is structurally necessary to at least one other.
2. **Cascades** — small actions produce delayed, large, sometimes irreversible consequences.
3. **Quiet wonder** — the swamp is not a battleground; it is a working system that rewards patient observation.
4. **Stewardship over conquest** — the player's tools are intervention and observation, not dominion.

**Tone register:**
- Calm. Slightly hushed. Naturalist, not didactic.
- The voice of a field guide left open on a porch table — informed, gentle, never lecturing.
- Awe without sentimentality. The mosquito gets the same respectful treatment as the heron.
- Educational without being a textbook. Every species description should make the player like that species a little more, even the unlovable ones.

**What the tone explicitly avoids:**
- Cute-mascot anthropomorphism. Frogs do not have names or personalities.
- Doom-mongering. Pollution and drought are presented as natural and human-driven phenomena, not moral failings.
- Game-y reward feedback. No achievements, no XP, no level-ups. Discovery is its own reward.
- Hard-science jargon in primary surfaces. Latin names appear in inspector tooltips only.

**Visual tonal language:**
- Muted, naturalistic palette: cypress green, mud brown, slate water, sunset amber for events.
- Sprites stylised but anatomically recognisable — flat-shaded, gentle outlines, not pixel art and not photoreal.
- Motion is slow and deliberate; nothing twitches, nothing flashes.
- Overlays are translucent and respect the underlying scene; data does not shout over the world.

## WORLD-BUILDING / CONCEPTS

**The Swamp as a Place**

The simulated environment is a temperate Southeastern North American freshwater swamp, modelled loosely on the Okefenokee and the Atchafalaya basins. It is a single contiguous habitat seen from above: a winding water channel between mudbanks, dotted with cypress knees and sawgrass islands, threaded with floating mats of duckweed and lily pads. It is a place that is wetter than land and drier than a lake — a transitional zone where life is dense precisely because conditions are unstable.

The swamp has no geographical name and no fictional history. It is an archetype, not a setting. Players bring their own associations — Louisiana bayou, Florida Everglades, Georgia blackwater — and the simulation does not contradict any of them.

**The Five Trophic Layers**

The world is built in five strata, presented to the player as an explicit conceptual ladder:

1. **Producers** — the green base. Sunlight becomes biomass. Algae, duckweed, cattails, lilies, cypress, sawgrass. They are the longest-lived agents and the most abundant.
2. **Primary Consumers** — the grazers and filterers. Mosquito larvae, tadpoles, snails, crayfish, turtles, ducks. They convert plants into mobile protein.
3. **Secondary Consumers** — the working hunters. Frogs, dragonflies, catfish, bass, water snakes, raccoons. The visible action layer; most player attention lives here.
4. **Apex Predators** — the few. Heron, alligator, otter, osprey. Rare, slow-reproducing, structurally critical.
5. **Decomposers & Cyclers** — the unsung. Bacteria (invisible), vultures, fireflies, beavers (as engineers). They close the loop and return matter to the producers. Removing them stops the world.

Each layer is a chapter in the visual language: producers are static or slow-drifting, consumers move with purpose, predators wait, decomposers work at the edges.

**The Seasons**

Time is cyclical. A simulated year passes through four seasons of equal length. Spring brings rain, runoff, and breeding. Summer brings heat, peak insect activity, and algae growth. Autumn brings die-back and migration. Winter brings cold-blooded torpor and the absence of migratory species. The seasons are characters: they are not toggles, they are forces the world responds to.

**Events and Disturbance**

Disturbance is not failure. Drought, flood, fire, and pollution are part of any healthy wetland's long history. The simulation treats them as recurring forces that shape the system over time. The player's interventions — adding species, removing species, triggering events — are framed as participation in this disturbance regime, not as a stewardship pass/fail test.

**The Player's Identity**

The player is not named. There is no avatar. There are no NPCs. The implied position of the player is that of a naturalist with godlike but consequence-bearing power: able to remove a species or trigger a flood at will, but unable to undo what unfolds afterward without explicit intervention. The simulation treats the player as a thoughtful adult — curious, slightly concerned, willing to wait.

**Guided Scenarios as Short Stories**

The five guided scenarios — Remove the Alligator, Mosquito Explosion, Algae Bloom, Beaver Dam, Drought Year — are structured as short ecological stories with a beginning (intervention), a middle (observable cascade), and an end (a summary that names the chain of cause and effect). They are the closest the product comes to authored narrative; everything else is emergent.

**Long-Term Vision**

Future content beyond the launch scope may add other biomes (mangrove, peat bog, vernal pool), seasons-of-the-year cycles longer than one calendar year, climate-change parameter sweeps, and multi-biome corridors. The launch scope deliberately does none of this — depth in one swamp matters more than breadth across many.

## Addendum — Vibrant Cartoon Look & Feel (UC-015, UC-016, UC-017)

The swamp is a *living children's picture-book*. The simulator is no longer a field of coloured dots; it is a tiny ecosystem theatre where every creature is a recognisable cartoon performer.

### Visual pillar
A frog reads as a frog at first glance. A heron stalks. A dragonfly shimmers. An alligator's tail curls in the sun. The user should be able to look at any patch of canvas, name three species without opening the inspector, and feel the swamp *breathing* even when nothing dramatic is happening.

### Motion pillar
Stillness is never frozen. Idle creatures pulse, sway, blink, hover. Moving creatures walk, swim, or fly in the right direction with the right gait — herons step, fish glide, ospreys flap. Locomotion changes when the environment changes: a frog that hops onto a lily pad stops swimming and starts breathing.

### UI pillar
The HUD shares the world's visual language: rounded buttons, hand-drawn icons, soft shadows, the same saturated swamp palette. Buttons squash on press. Scenario cards are illustrated. Save slots look like polaroid frames. The interface is part of the picture-book, not a separate engineering tool.

### Constraints
- Cartoon, not cute-noisy: thick outline, two-tone shading, no photoreal textures.
- Performance: must hold 30 fps with 400 animated agents on a baseline laptop.
- Accessibility: respects `prefers-reduced-motion`; every interactive control keyboard-focusable.
- No regressions: every screen, control, and event from UC-001…UC-014 keeps working.

## Addendum — Living Engineers, Wild Terrain, and Ecosystem Depth (UC-018..UC-021)

### Beaver engineer pillar
The beaver is the swamp's most visible engineer. When a beaver decides to dam a channel, the player watches the entire construction story: the swim to the cut-site, the log carry — one branch at a time — the mud packing, the final patting-down that seals the structure. The dam is not a scenario prop but a living, destructible object. Storms batter it; drought weakens the mortar; the beaver returns to patch cracks. Other species colonise it: a heron uses the crest as a hunting platform, frogs broadcast from the upstream shallows, small fish school in the calm pond behind it. When the player demolishes the dam the water rushes out in a visible breach cascade and the downstream ecosystem recalibrates. Multiple dams can coexist, each at a different stage of construction or decay, each supporting a micro-ecosystem.

### Procedural terrain pillar
No two runs of the SwampSimulator should feel identical. A seeded Perlin-noise generator carves the canvas into six zone types — deep water (dark teal channels), shallow wading flats (light teal), exposed mudbanks (warm brown), grassy land patches (dark olive), dry sand bars (ochre), and cypress-tree island stands (deep shadow-green). The boundaries are organic: no tile grid, no hard edge, only the soft meander of a real bayou. Organisms respect their zone affinity: a snapping turtle glides through deep water but basks on a sunny mudbank; a raccoon forages on the grass patch but will not wade into the channel. Semi-aquatic species transition visually at zone boundaries — the alligator's swim-drag posture shifts to its terrestrial lumber the moment it hauls onto the bank. Seasonal flooding and drought morph the terrain in real time.

### Plant-life pillar
Plants are not decoration. They are animated agents with their own life cycle, zone preferences, and seasonal states. Lily pads bob with sine-wave offsets per patch, so the surface breathes unevenly rather than in lockstep. Cattails sway in the wind; cypress canopies rustle in short 4-frame loops; algae patches pulse as their population expands and contracts. When a beaver harvests a reed for dam building, the plant shakes and pops out of existence with a cartoon puff. In winter the whole plant layer desaturates and shrinks; in summer it crowds the shallow zones with dense green. The plant layer fills in the visual silences between animal events, ensuring the canvas is always alive.

### Depth and atmosphere pillar
The canvas has depth. The far background holds muted cypress silhouettes and a sky gradient; the terrain layer sits on top; then water with ripple particles; then plants; then animals; then the particle overlay (splashes, firefly blinks, rain, confetti). Time of day shades everything: dawn tints the canvas warm orange; noon bleaches it bright; dusk burns amber; night cloaks it in deep indigo, pierced only by firefly blink-patterns and bioluminescent algae glow. Weather events add layers: rain streaks fall at a realistic slant; a storm flickers lightning across the background; autumn fog softens all edges. Predation leaves a brief location-specific splash burst. Death leaves a two-second luminous fade. Milestone achievements — first dam completed, first heron breeding pair — trigger a canvas-wide confetti shower to celebrate the ecosystem's resilience.

### Extended constraints
- Terrain generation MUST be seeded and reproducible; the seed MUST be stored in save state.
- Plant sprite-sheets MUST use the same row/col schema as animal sheets to avoid dual renderers.
- Particle and ambient effects MUST degrade gracefully under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- All new features MUST maintain ≥ 30 fps at 1280×800 with ≥ 20 agents and full plant coverage.
- UC-001..UC-017 MUST remain fully functional and visually intact after this addendum is implemented.

## Second-Pass Quality Addendum (edge and simple cases)

To improve implementation safety and reduce implicit assumptions, the narrative now explicitly includes low-complexity and failure-adjacent scenarios:

- **Simple-case baseline**: on a fresh run with no active event and low density, the swamp still feels alive through subtle plant motion, sparse ripples, and stable daylight tinting (no milestone or scenario trigger required).
- **Zero-builder case**: if no beaver reaches a planned build site, the UI should communicate "construction paused" rather than implying hidden progress.
- **Invalid-seed case**: malformed or out-of-range seed inputs normalize to a valid positive integer and are surfaced consistently in URL/hash and visible seed chip.
- **Reduced-motion case**: the world remains informative when animation intensity is reduced, using static cues (icons, labels, color-state) instead of motion-only signaling.
- **Sparse-population atmosphere case**: ambient activity indicator remains visible at low population and scales upward without clipping at high population.
- **Load-recovery case**: regenerable visual fields (terrain map buffers, animation internals) are rebuilt from durable values after load, avoiding corruption from oversized snapshots.
