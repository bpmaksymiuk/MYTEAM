# Narrative — Species role text

Naturalist tone. One short sentence per species, suitable for the inspector "Role" line.

## Producers
- Algae: Floating green growth that turns sunlight into food and oxygen for the swamp.
- Duckweed: A small floating plant that shelters tadpoles and feeds turtles.
- Cattails: Tall reeds that line the shore and host insect larvae.
- Water lilies: Pads that shade the water and attract pollinators.
- Cypress trees: Anchor the swamp; their roots stabilise mud and slow flooding.
- Sawgrass: Stiff blades that filter water and support nesting birds.

## Primary consumers
- Tadpoles: Young frogs that graze algae and become a key prey species as adults.
- Snails: Slow grazers that keep algae in check and feed many small carnivores.
- Mosquito larvae: Aquatic young of the mosquito, food for fish and dragonfly nymphs.
- Deer: Browses cypress saplings and shoreline plants; visits to drink each day.
- Turtle: Omnivorous grazer; eats duckweed, fallen fruit, and small invertebrates.
- Mullet: A schooling fish that grazes algae and sediment.

## Secondary consumers
- Bullfrog: Eats mosquitoes and dragonflies; bioindicator of water quality.
- Largemouth bass: A predatory fish that controls smaller fish and tadpoles.
- Dragonfly: Hunts mosquitoes in the air; nymphs hunt them in the water.
- Heron: Wades the shallows; takes frogs, fish, and crayfish.
- Crayfish: Scavenger and small predator of the muddy bottom.
- Otter: Agile predator of fish and crayfish; sensitive to pollution.
- Snake: Eats frogs and small fish along the shore.

## Apex predators
- Alligator: Apex predator and engineer; its hole holds water through droughts.
- Florida panther: Stalks deer and otters along the swamp's edge.
- Barred owl: Night hunter of small mammals and snakes.
- Osprey (migratory): Plunges for fish in the open water during warm seasons.

## Decomposers and engineers
- Vulture: Cleans up carcasses; recycles biomass into nutrients.
- Firefly: Adult signals at dusk; larvae prey on snails.
- Beaver: Engineers the landscape; its dam raises water and expands wetlands.
